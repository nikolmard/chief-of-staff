Connecting the dots

A business works like a body. Every part can be strong on its own, but it still won't perform without what holds it together.

The head

Your vision and strategy

Where the company is going and why.

The skeleton

Structure, processes and tools

The tech stack, the org chart, the way things are meant to run.

The muscles

Your teams and functions

Sales, product, operations: the people doing the work.

The nervous system

Information and decisions

What reaches whom, when, and in what form.

The connective tissue

Mutual understanding between the people involved

It makes sure all parts have what they need and they pull in the same direction. You don't notice it until it's missing.

As the business grows, the lack of connective tissue becomes more and more visible.

New people join, meetings multiply, following up becomes impossible, and things fall through the cracks.

Solutions that fail:

Doing everything yourself

The head tries to do the muscles' job too, until the bottleneck stifles growth completely.

Delegating everything

The muscles move on their own, and you lose the overview of what's actually going on.

Adding headcount

More muscle, but nothing new connecting it to the rest.

AI automation

Speeds up the back end, but hits its limit with the soft part of running a business: people working with people.

An occasional town hall doesn't replace regular two-way communication. A last-minute email asking for updates isn't alignment. And a monthly Friday happy hour won't create a healthy culture if the rest of the time people are fighting fires that could have been prevented.

People first

Most people start with the business. I start with people, because people are business.

It's rarely a bad process or a technical fault that causes bottlenecks. It's misunderstood situations and unmet needs. So I build the connective tissue first, and then everything else has something to hold on to.

  1. I take time to understand you.

    What you're struggling with. Who you are as a leader and as a human being. What you need, what's important to you, what worries you, what gives you energy, and how you work with others.

  2. Then your leadership team and your board.

    The dynamics between them, what motivates each person, and what's important to them. How each department head understands the vision and their role in it. What each of them needs from you and from each other to work as a team, not just a sum of brilliant individuals.

  3. Then mutual understanding.

    You understand what your board and leadership need, and they understand you and each other. This is the connective tissue. Alignment comes quickly when people see each other as whole human beings, with feelings, motivations and needs.

  4. Then strategy and operations.

    With that foundation in place, we build the rest:

    • Get what's in your head on paper: what needs to happen, who needs to be involved, how and when.
    • Test it with the right people and information, so it holds.
    • Turn it into a single source of truth, with communication lines built around it so people stay aligned.
    • Set up operating rhythms with two-way communication, not just one-way information sharing.

A large offshore energy company had one big goal, and people in three regions could not see how it applied to their day. I helped each region align on six things it could really deliver, gave each an owner, and set up check-ins to clear obstacles.

40+ leaders in one room.

Operations and support teams barely talked. I ran a multi-month process that brought together leaders from three operating assets plus Technical, Supply Chain, People and executives. Then I set up a shared online space and a regular meeting so it would last. Each asset left with its own improvement program.

Built from nothing, with no authority.
